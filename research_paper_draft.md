To assess the effectiveness of the proposed Multi-Agent Self-Reflective Agentic RAG Framework, multiple evaluation metrics are employed to measure retrieval performance, response quality, factual reliability, reasoning capability, and confidence estimation. The selected metrics provide a comprehensive assessment of the framework from both retrieval and generation perspectives.

5.1 Core Verification Metrics
A. Reflection Quality Score
The Reflection Quality Score measures the effectiveness of the Reflection Agent in identifying weaknesses, mistakes, ambiguities, and missing information in the generated response. This score is based on the number and severity of detected issues.

A higher reflection score indicates that the self-reflection mechanism is functioning effectively and the model is capable of self-critique.

B. Fact Verification Score
The Fact Verification Score measures the proportion of factual claims in the generated response that are supported by retrieved evidence or domain knowledge.

Fact Verification Score = Verified Claims / Total Claims

Higher values indicate stronger factual consistency between generated responses and the knowledge base
C. Consistency Score
The Consistency Score evaluates whether multiple generated responses agree at the semantic level. This metric detects conflicting entities, dates, numbers, and names across sampled responses.

Consistency Score = 1 - (Contradictions Found / Total Response Pairs)

A higher consistency score represents better agreement across multiple samples and stronger output stability.

D. Reasoning Score
The Reasoning Score measures the logical validity of the response by evaluating whether conclusions are supported by appropriate evidence and reasoning steps. This metric is generated by the Reason Checker Agent and reflects the reasoning capability of the proposed framework.

A higher reasoning score indicates more rigorous logical structure and better evidential support for conclusions.

E. Semantic Similarity Score
The Semantic Similarity Score evaluates the degree of semantic agreement among multiple generated responses using embedding-based cosine similarity. This metric complements consistency checking by capturing meaning-level alignment.

Semantic Similarity = (1 / n(n-1)) * sum of cosine_similarity(response_i, response_j) for all pairs

Higher values indicate stronger semantic convergence and reduced response variability.
5.2 Secondary Evaluation Metrics
F. Retrieval Relevance Score
The Retrieval Relevance Score evaluates the semantic similarity between retrieved document chunks and the user query. It reflects the effectiveness of the Retrieval Agent in identifying useful contextual information.

A higher relevance score generally leads to more accurate and evidence-grounded responses.

G. Hallucination Rate
Hallucination Rate quantifies the percentage of generated responses containing unsupported or factually incorrect statements.

Hallucination Rate = (Unsupported Claims / Total Claims) × 100%

A lower hallucination rate indicates better factual grounding and improved reliability.

H. Confidence Calibration Score
Confidence Calibration measures how well the predicted confidence score corresponds to the actual correctness of generated responses. Well-calibrated confidence scores improve transparency and enable users to better interpret the reliability of system outputs.

I. Response Latency
Response Latency measures the total time required to generate and verify a response.

Latency = t_output - t_input

Although multiple verification agents introduce additional computation, latency is evaluated to ensure practical usability.
5.3 Aggregated Reliability Assessment
J. Overall Reliability Score
The proposed framework computes a unified reliability score by aggregating the outputs of all verification agents using weighted combination:

R = w_f * F + w_c * C + w_r * R + w_s * S + w_si * SI

where:

F = Fact Verification Score (weight: 0.30)
C = Consistency Score (weight: 0.15)
R = Reasoning Score (weight: 0.20)
S = Semantic Similarity Score (weight: 0.15)
SI = Self-Reflection Quality Score (weight: 0.20)
The weights satisfy: w_f + w_c + w_r + w_s + w_si = 1.0

A response is considered reliable if: R ≥ T

where T represents the predefined reliability threshold (default: 0.70).

K. Confidence Level Classification
Based on the overall reliability score, responses are classified into confidence levels:

Very High Confidence: R ≥ 0.90 – High likelihood of correctness and strong evidence support
High Confidence: 0.80 ≤ R < 0.90 – Good evidence support with minor concerns
Medium Confidence: 0.70 ≤ R < 0.80 – Moderate evidence but some uncertainties
Low Confidence: 0.50 ≤ R < 0.70 – Weak evidence or contradictions detected
Very Low Confidence: R < 0.50 – Significant concerns about factual accuracy or reasoning
